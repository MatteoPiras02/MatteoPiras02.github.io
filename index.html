M.S. thesis · NYU Tandon

Astra: a highly dynamic quadruped

December 2025 – present · Advisor: Prof. Chris Clark · Co-advisor: Prof. Ludovic Righetti

Astra is a 12-degree-of-freedom, ~17 kg quadruped I am designing and building to run, jump and eventually backflip, at about a third of the cost of a comparable commercial robot.

Everything on Astra is designed and manufactured from scratch. The structural parts are printed in PPA-CF, a carbon-fiber-reinforced engineering polymer, with TPU feet. Every joint is driven by an actuator I designed: a large brushless motor with a 10:1 cycloidal reducer whose cycloidal disks are custom-machined, giving the torque, speed and backdrivability that dynamic motion needs.

I also wrote the control software, with safety monitoring built in from the start, and a physics simulation of the robot that I validated against the real hardware.

Today Astra stands up on its own and controls its body roll, pitch and yaw. Walking comes next, then dynamic gaits.

10:1cycloidal reduction
0.006°mean static position error

The robot for ROB-UY 3303 Robot Motion and Planning

Fall 2026 · Course Assistant for Prof. Chris Clark

I designed the robot that every team in the course builds and programs during the semester: a differential-drive platform with wheel encoders, an IMU and a 360° LiDAR, run by an ESP32 that students control over WiFi from a Python GUI.

  • Designed the chassis and electronics layout for easy assembly by students.
  • Wrote the ESP32 firmware and the Python control and telemetry GUI.
  • Wrote a 19-page illustrated assembly manual.
  • Designed and delivered 15 kits, one per team, for about 30 students.

Course project · NYU Tandon

Localization and coverage for an autonomous cleaning robot

Spring 2026 · with Berinay Demirci

A localization system for a small differential-drive robot in a known indoor map. Wheel-encoder and gyroscope odometry is corrected by a particle filter that compares LiDAR distances with the walls expected from the map. The position estimate then drives a coverage path across the room.

2.90 cmmean localization error
3.11 cmRMSE over 20 trials
~70%coverage in about 2 min

B.S. thesis · University of Padua

Bio-inspired quadruped, and the two before it

2024 – 2025

My bachelor's thesis robot was the third quadruped I built, after two earlier personal prototypes. Its leg design and gait were inspired by animal biomechanics.

I implemented the inverse kinematics in C on a Raspberry Pi, with posture and balance control on an Arduino. Each robot taught me something that went into the next one, and the lessons from all three shaped Astra.
